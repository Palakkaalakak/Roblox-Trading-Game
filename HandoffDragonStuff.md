The goal is a Blox Fruits-style West Dragon: a button-triggered transformation VFX (done, still being tuned), then a green western dragon model that replaces the player after the blast (not built), double-tap-Space flight, and an R-key fireball. The user wants the VFX near-indistinguishable from the original and is very credit-limited, so work in small verified steps, one combined patch and one screenshot per change, and never claim a match without a same-moment side-by-side against the reference. The effect lives in two places: `ReplicatedStorage.DragonWestVFXCore` (a ModuleScript where every visual is a pure function of time `t` in seconds, with `M.build(parent, groundPos)`, `M.step(state, t, cameraPos)`, `M.destroy(state)`, `M.DURATION = 4.4`, and `M.ASSETS`) and `StarterPlayer.StarterPlayerScripts.DragonWestTransformVFX` (a LocalScript driver that adds a "West Transform" button, fires `ReplicatedStorage.DragonWestTransformEvent`, plays the module in real time on RenderStepped, and drives camera FOV punch-in/out, a white flash with a black ink-splat ImageLabel, and camera shake). Nothing outside these was touched, and the user's other scripts must not be modified. Studio session IDs change whenever Studio restarts, so always call `list_roblox_studios` first. Reference material is in `docs/research/dragon-vfx/` (README with a 12-group effect inventory and a beat sheet) and in `%TEMP%\dragonref\` (video `full.webm` = YouTube XsJS2qqogYM, `v_XO3j1vVj7vo.webm` = Eastern/Western showcase with the West transform at about 6:52-6:55 source time, `v_8GhIppI299k.webm` = second angle with a clean night-time West transform around 14-16s, and frames in `nat2/f_NNN.png` at 12fps from 412.0s of the XO3j1vVj7vo video, where frame 7 and 10 show the formation and frame 22 the crack/ray beat). ## What the West transformation looks like (verified frame by frame, about 2.7 s)

A camera punch-in with a red/orange/yellow shard starburst and a white triangle, a ground bullseye half-disc, then a matte black ball (a hemisphere resting on the ground) grows while exactly three thick, flat, solid yellow bands (orange-red soft edge, no streak texture) wrap it with clear black gaps between them, the bottom one flaring slightly like a brim only in the early reference frames. After about 0.7 s the solid bands become thin glowing streak rings (one hugging the base, one mid-sphere, a halo ring on top). Short curled purple/magenta flare beams burst from the ball early and fade by about 0.9 s. Wide cream-yellow translucent tendril ribbons sweep far out around it. There are red vertical streaks and thin diagonal lines on the dome, orange embers and flame slivers on the ground and rim, then jagged glowing orange-yellow cracks spread over the ball, thick yellow rays with orange outlines shoot out, the ball flashes solid yellow, then white-out plus a black ink splat, a cel-shaded orange/yellow fireball, black smoke, navy debris cubes, a lava fire ring with yellow flame tongues, and the winged dragon appears in the fire. Colors: yellow (255,214,20), orange (255,118,0), red (225,35,15), pink (255,40,190), purple (150,50,230). Things the user explicitly rejected and that must not return: a bottom brim/pink/purple base ring under the ball, a solid pink/purple sphere moving beside it, ground ink splats, capsule/cylinder-walled dome (it must be a ball), cracks that follow the camera (they must be attached to the ball surface), stray black spikes, any generic Roblox/stock sparkle sprites, washed-out brightness, and anything that clips through the ball or ground. The ball radius is 25.5 studs (R=25.5, scaled 1.5x from 17) and all other sizes were scaled with it. Two or more golden rays must point toward the player camera so that side is not barren (3 camera-facing rays are now built: front rays use a live direction of camera*0.55 + side + up).

## Technical lessons learned (these cost a lot of time)

Screenshots: `mcp__Roblox_Studio__screen_capture` works reliably only in Edit mode with `camera_position`/`look_at_position`, with the Studio window in front and not minimized (use the computer-use tools to bring it forward if needed; do not close the real window). It returns a blank/dark image or the game's loading overlay in Play mode or when Studio is not visible; retry once. Every execute_luau call goes through a wrapper that resets camera type to Scriptable, which locks the user's editor camera; after any screenshot or camera-touching call run `task.delay(4, function() workspace.CurrentCamera.CameraType = Enum.CameraType.Fixed end)` (it persists and unlocks movement). Hide game UI for captures with `StarterGui.ShowDevelopmentGui = false`, then restore it to true and tell the user (they asked for it back on). Test scenes go at y=800 in a `VFXPose` folder and must be deleted afterwards; also delete any stray `DragonVFXBloom` BloomEffect from Lighting (a leftover one made the whole game look washed out), and never leave the camera Scriptable. Edit-mode-only calls (asset insert, script edits) fail with "not available in Play mode"; stop play first with `start_stop_play is_start=false`. Large uploads: serve a local folder with `python -m http.server <port> --bind 127.0.0.1` and call `upload_image` with `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/file.png` (it returns `rbxassetid://` ids; the processing takes up to ~20 s before textures render, otherwise they show as plain white); kill the server afterwards. Creator Store "Decal" ids are not image ids (they fail ContentProvider fetch); resolve with `InsertService:LoadAsset` on the Server datamodel to get the real image id. Textures: bake the edge color into transparent pixels (otherwise dark fringes show), draw cel-style crisp art with Pillow, and view the preview before uploading. Beam textures map across width, so rotate band textures accordingly; Beam `Brightness` plus `LightEmission=0` keeps colors vivid without additive washout, and `FaceCamera=false` lets a beam lie flat. SurfaceGui plates on thin parts were unreliable for cracks; BillboardGuis work but follow the camera; geometry (thin Neon Part segments laid along random walks on the sphere) works and stays attached. Neon material hides Decals/Textures; Highlight outline gives the yellow rim. Roblox mesh/anim uploads are not possible through the MCP (only `upload_image`), so a rigged mesh model cannot be uploaded by Claude; the user must import via File > Import 3D. The user is extremely sensitive to anything that looks cheap or different from the reference, wants real screenshots shown side by side, and wants no excuses. Use the caveman style replies and be ultra credit efficient (their usage is nearly exhausted): no re-reading files, one patch per round, short answers.

## Retrospective: how to do VFX recreation faster and better (life hacks)

The transformation took dozens of rounds mostly because of process mistakes, not Roblox limits. Do these instead. (1) Study the reference once, properly, before building: pull 12 fps frames of the whole effect, build 3x3 contact sheets, list every distinct element per beat with timestamps and sampled hex colors, and write that as a checklist first. I built from thumbnails and memory, so I missed whole effect groups (ground bullseye, tendrils, purple flares, camera punch-in) and had to rebuild several times. Look at 2-3 different camera angles of the same effect early; one angle misled me (the pink "splats" and the "sphere" were background smears). (2) Build deterministic from the start: make every visual a pure function of time `t` (`M.step(state, t)`), so any moment can be frozen and compared to the reference frame at the same moment, in Edit mode, with a fixed camera that matches the reference framing. Real-time Play-mode screenshots and slow-motion stills were nearly useless and cost many calls. (3) Always compare side by side programmatically (a small PIL script that crops the reference and my capture to the same box and tiles them) and send that image to the user, instead of describing it. (4) Match the reference's art style before tuning numbers: Blox Fruits uses flat, hard-edged, cel-shaded, fully saturated shapes (solid bands with a red-orange rim, jagged cracks, cel fire puffs), not soft glows. Stock Roblox sprites, Creator Store "VFX" decals, and photo textures never matched; drawing my own crisp textures in Pillow (preview them, bleed edge colors into transparent pixels, upload through a local http server) matched far better, and for lines/cracks, plain thin Neon Part geometry attached to the shape beats any texture trick. (5) Pick the right Roblox primitive per element: Beams with `FaceCamera=false` for flat swept rings and ribbons, Trails for streaks, Beam `Brightness` for vivid color without washout, Parts and WedgeParts for rays, billboards only for camera-facing puffs, never SurfaceGuis on thin transparent parts for important elements. (6) Preload textures and wait about 20 s after upload before judging a capture; white shapes always mean "not loaded yet". (7) Scale once: define one radius constant `R` and express every other size as a multiple, so a size change is one edit (I scaled by hand twice). (8) Batch edits: one `execute_luau` that applies all replacements with a fails list, rebuilds the pose, and ends with a single screenshot; avoid re-reading source, and check a replacement string exists before assuming it applied (a failed `rep` silently skipped patches several times). (9) Keep the user's editor safe: reset the camera type after every capture, restore UI and delete test folders/lights/bloom every time, never leave anything in Lighting, and do not run desktop-control tools that could close or minimize the real Studio window. (10) Know what cannot be done from the MCP (mesh/animation upload, real rigging of imported meshes beyond Motor6D setup, watching real-time motion) and tell the user up front, ask for the one thing needed (e.g. a screen recording or an imported model) instead of guessing. (11) Plan the model choice and effort level early: use a stronger model at medium effort for the visual comparison rounds, a cheaper one for mechanical edits, and stop and ask before long blind loops when usage is low.
